An institution in Japan

Quiet authority, built to last for decades.

Toyota introduced the Century in 1967 as Japan’s definitive chauffeur-driven automobile. Its name commemorated the 100th anniversary of the birth of Sakichi Toyoda, founder of the Toyota Group—a fitting origin for a car intended to embody a century of Japanese achievement, craftsmanship, and quiet dignity.

For its first 30 years, the Century evolved carefully rather than chasing fashion. Hand-finished in limited numbers, it became the discreet choice of the Imperial Household, senior officials, corporate leaders, celebrities, and other passengers who valued privacy and composure over display.

The GZG50 shown here arrived in 1997 as only the second generation of the Century. It remained in production through 2017—an extraordinary 21-year run—during which just over 9,000 examples were built. Its restrained styling and fundamental character changed remarkably little because Toyota treated continuity as a virtue: the Century was not meant to follow the luxury market, but to stand apart from it.

At its center was the 5.0-liter 1GZ-FE, the first V12 fitted to a Japanese production passenger car. Around it, Toyota built a car around the rear passenger: exceptional quietness, a supple ride, soft-close doors, low sills, deep wool upholstery, carefully matched wood, and controls designed to make long journeys effortless. Even its paintwork reflected the same philosophy, with extensive hand-finishing and as many as seven coats applied to achieve its depth.

This Century

A rare survivor in an equally rare color.

What makes this Century remarkable is how faithfully it has survived. It remains unmodified and exceptionally close to the way it left the factory, with some original protective wrappers still in place and a cabin that has never been altered or smoked in.

VIP rear compartment

A rear leg rest uses a pass-through in the front passenger seat, giving the principal passenger room to stretch out.

Century-specific technology

Voice-controlled navigation, VCD rear entertainment, Toyota MONET connectivity, and a Century-branded Sony dictation Walkman.

Thoughtful access

Low door sills, picture-frame windows, and power-assisted soft-close doors express luxury through ease rather than ornament.

Factory-correct stewardship

It has been carefully maintained using genuine Toyota parts, many sourced directly from Japan. Japanese manuals, maintenance records, inspection paperwork, and original literature remain with the car.
